# Source Register

Each entry states both the admitted claim and the boundary on that claim.
Institutional pages support observing-system descriptions; primary papers
support technical mechanisms and quantitative results.

| ID | Source | Admitted use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | [NASA Perpetual Ocean 2](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5479) | model-assimilative visualization of currents at multiple depths with temperature and salinity renderings | visualization is not a direct instantaneous photograph or a closed heat budget |
| O2 | [WHOI currents, gyres, and eddies](https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-topics/how-the-ocean-works/ocean-circulation/currents-gyres-eddies/) | circulation controls and public vocabulary | relative controls vary by depth, scale, and region |
| O3 | [NSIDC Arctic weather and climate](https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/arctic-weather-and-climate/science-arctic-weather-and-climate) | Arctic basin geometry and Atlantic heat transport | inflow alone does not determine the full Arctic energy budget |
| O4 | [Gutierrez-Villanueva et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1175/JPO-D-19-0266.1) | ACC fronts inhibit exchange while eddies carry poleward heat; Drake Passage is an enhanced eddy-flux region | upper-ocean estimate; the ACC is leaky, not a material wall |
| O5 | [Hutchinson et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-15-0554.1) | coupled Drake-closure experiment with polar warming and Antarctic sea-ice retreat | idealized model, not a feasible intervention or dynamic grounded-ice forecast |
| O6 | [Goldner et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13597) | Antarctic glaciation and CO2 drawdown can explain circulation changes better than gateway opening alone | paleoclimate attribution does not directly predict modern closure |
| O7 | [Touzeau et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemgeo.2021.120171) | proxy chronology for Drake throughflow and ACC onset | timing remains debated and cannot isolate every causal contribution |
| O8 | [Orsi et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0967-0637(95)00021-W) | hydrographic definitions of major ACC fronts | climatological fronts are not fixed material contours |
| O9 | [Sokolov and Rintoul 2009](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008JC005108) | persistent ACC front branches and enhanced gradients | mean paths hide meandering and branch changes |
| O10 | [Thompson et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018RG000624) | Circumpolar Deep Water, slope fronts, shelf exchange, and regional barriers | heterogeneous pathways do not define one circumpolar melt rate |
| O11 | [Moorman et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec7443) | modeled Antarctic coastal heat budget and ice-related sinks | fraction depends on forcing, configuration, and represented ice processes |
| O12 | [TEOS-10](https://www.teos-10.org/) | thermodynamically consistent seawater property framework | constant-density and heat-capacity equations here are pedagogical approximations |
| O13 | [Marshall and Speer 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo1391) | Southern Ocean winds, overturning, sloping density surfaces, and eddy transport | zonal-mean framework does not specify local exchange |
| O14 | [Bennetts et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022RG000781) | three-dimensional Southern Ocean synthesis | individual pathways require regional observations |
| O15 | [Spence et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014GL060613) | poleward-wind-shift experiment and regional subsurface warming | response is sensitive to geometry and model configuration |
| O16 | [Youngs et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-026-01975-6) | competing ice-shelf meltwater feedbacks | regional signs are not universal constants |
| O17 | [Jenkins 2025](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-marine-040323-074354) | hierarchy of barriers between offshore heat and basal melting | no one pathway or parameterization fits every ice shelf |
| O18 | [NOAA Indo-Pacific Warm Pool](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/featured-images/warm-pool-indo-pacific-ocean-has-almost-doubled-size-changing-global) | location and climatic role of the persistent warm pool | footprint depends on threshold, season, dataset, and baseline |
| O19 | [NOAA Western Hemisphere Warm Pool](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/phod/research/tav/awp/) | seasonal warm-pool geography split by Central America | diagnostic boundary is not a material contour |
| O20 | [Gentemann et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016GL071039) | satellite characterization of the Northeast Pacific marine heatwave | surface anomaly does not quantify full-depth heat content or one cause |
| O21 | [Jackson et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL078971) | subsurface persistence of Blob-related heat | regional remnant is not universal marine-heatwave behavior |
| O22 | [Palmer et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2019.00416) | ocean heat observing systems and Arctic Atlantic Water below the halocline | heat release varies with inflow region and stratification |
| J1 | [Kaspi et al. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25793) | Jupiter jet depth from gravity constraints | inferred depth is not directly imaged flow |
| J2 | [Li et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-020-1009-3) | Juno microwave pressure sensitivity | temperature and absorber abundance require retrieval |
| J3 | [Fletcher et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JE006858) | belt/zone contrast reversal near the 5–10 bar jovicline | microwave brightness is jointly sensitive to temperature and composition |
| S1 | [García-Melendo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2011.07.005) | Saturn winds at wavelength-dependent levels and thermal-wind comparison | cloud tracers do not directly measure deep heat transport |
| S2 | [Galanti et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57790-x) | Saturn jet depth and gravity-inversion uncertainty | solution depends on interior structure and allowed wind profiles |
