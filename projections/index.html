EXPERIMENT 01 · PROJECTION BAKEOFF

Where should the
world ocean begin?

Most world maps preserve continents by cutting the ocean. This experiment reverses that priority and asks which projection best preserves the geography of reservoirs, anomalies, currents, and gates.

Leading hypothesisPELAGOS

Equal-area, ocean-led, and deliberately weakest over the Sahara—not yet adopted as the atlas default.

The test

  1. 01Do not cut a major ocean pathway.
  2. 02Preserve area when comparing heatmass scale.
  3. 03Keep the warm pool, Arctic approaches, and ACC legible together.
  4. 04Put unavoidable distortion and seams over land.
01

Spilhaus

Continuity benchmark

What it proves

The world ocean can become the continuous subject rather than the space between continents.

Where it fails this atlas

The conformal square preserves local angles, not area. A heatmass can look larger or smaller because of projection distortion.

02

Oceanic Goode

Area benchmark

What it proves

Relative heatmass area can remain honest across the entire world.

Where it fails this atlas

Its lobes interrupt the ocean system—the very relationship OCEANLINES is trying to reveal.

03

Equal Earth

Flat-world benchmark

What it offers

A familiar flat map with honest relative area and much clearer heatmass silhouettes.

Where it fails this atlas

The full-height seam through Africa still divides Arctic and Southern Ocean pathways.

04

Pelagos

OCEANLINES global experiment

GLOBAL
What it offers

The Pacific heat reservoir leads, the ACC closes visibly around Antarctica, and the major ocean pathways stay away from the outer edge.

Declared weakness

Shape distortion increases toward Africa. The antipodal singular edge is placed at 20°N, 15°E in the Sahara.

EXPERIMENT 02 · SHAPE ATLAS

One ocean map cannot make every heatmass legible.

HEATPLATES gives each reservoir or anomaly a local Lambert equal-area panel. The view preserves each silhouette and its nearby coastlines while surrendering one continuous world.

Important

Every panel has its own zoom because the current boundaries are schematic. Compare shape and context—not footprint area.

EXPERIMENT 03 · PROVINCE ATLAS

What if the ocean read like a country?

This reference cartogram turns the classic 56 Longhurst surface provinces into the primary geography. The Pacific, Indian, and Atlantic become the broad body of the map; continents survive only as narrow contextual seams.

Cartogram, not projection

It preserves basin order and a readable regional grouping—not geographic area, distance, direction, exact adjacency, or exact contact with land.

The memorable layer: four-letter province codes work like state abbreviations. Hover or keyboard-focus any province in the full-size SVG for its complete name.

The scientific layer: colors identify Longhurst's four biomes—Polar, Westerlies, Trade Winds, and Coastal. These static shapes are a reference vocabulary; real ecological boundaries migrate.

The edition layer: this experiment explicitly uses the classic 56-code vocabulary, including separate OCAL and CCAL California provinces. The later 54-province revision should become a separate switch—not an invisible correction.

Open the complete 56-row province directory →Codes, full names, basin, biome, edition, and geometry status; usable without color or pointer interaction.

METHOD FIREWALL

New viewpoint,
established mathematics.

PELAGOS is an experimental aspect and cut policy, not a newly derived projection equation. It uses spherical Lambert azimuthal equal-area centered at 20°S, 165°W:

+proj=laea +lat_0=-20 +lon_0=-165 +R=1

Its antipode—and unavoidable singular boundary—is 20°N, 15°E. Placing that point in the Sahara moves the projection's greatest failure away from the Pacific warm pool, the Southern Ocean ring, and most major exchange pathways.

All four global maps use the same irregular geographic heat polygons, inset shelf contours, transient anomaly, pathways, gates, and checksum-pinned Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines. The polygon boundaries are schematic—not observed thresholds. These are projection tests, not heat observations or transport calculations.

The Province Atlas is different by design: it is original cartogram geometry built from the classic Longhurst province vocabulary, not transformed geographic polygons. It therefore supports recognition and comparison of the named parts, but no measurement of area, distance, direction, or transport. The static province system is a mean ecological reference whose natural boundaries move seasonally and interannually.
