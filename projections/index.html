EXPERIMENT 01 · PROJECTION BAKEOFF

Where should the
world ocean begin?

Most world maps preserve continents by cutting the ocean. This experiment reverses that priority and asks which projection best preserves the geography of reservoirs, anomalies, currents, and gates.

Leading hypothesisPELAGOS

Equal-area, ocean-led, and deliberately weakest over the Sahara—not yet adopted as the atlas default.

The test

  1. 01Do not cut a major ocean pathway.
  2. 02Preserve area when comparing heatmass scale.
  3. 03Keep the warm pool, Arctic approaches, and ACC legible together.
  4. 04Put unavoidable distortion and seams over land.
01

Spilhaus

Continuity benchmark

What it proves

The world ocean can become the continuous subject rather than the space between continents.

Where it fails this atlas

The conformal square preserves local angles, not area. A heatmass can look larger or smaller because of projection distortion.

02

Oceanic Goode

Area benchmark

What it proves

Relative heatmass area can remain honest across the entire world.

Where it fails this atlas

Its lobes interrupt the ocean system—the very relationship OCEANLINES is trying to reveal.

03

Pelagos

OCEANLINES experiment

LEADING
What it offers

The Pacific heat reservoir leads, the ACC closes visibly around Antarctica, and the major ocean pathways stay away from the outer edge.

Declared weakness

Shape distortion increases toward Africa. The antipodal singular edge is placed at 20°N, 15°E in the Sahara.

DECISION SURFACE

No projection wins everything.

PELAGOS leads because area and ocean continuity are more important to this atlas than familiar continental shapes.

METHOD FIREWALL

New viewpoint,
established mathematics.

PELAGOS is an experimental aspect and cut policy, not a newly derived projection equation. It uses spherical Lambert azimuthal equal-area centered at 20°S, 165°W:

+proj=laea +lat_0=-20 +lon_0=-165 +R=1

Its antipode—and unavoidable singular boundary—is 20°N, 15°E. Placing that point in the Sahara moves the projection's greatest failure away from the Pacific warm pool, the Southern Ocean ring, and most major exchange pathways.

All three maps use the same schematic fluid features and the same checksum-pinned Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines. They are projection tests, not heat observations or transport calculations.
